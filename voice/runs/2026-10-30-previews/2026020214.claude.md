# The Devils' power play meets the league's best kill in Vegas

Two of the better teams in the league meet for the first time this season. The Golden Knights are 9-4-1, second in the Pacific, and 6-2-0 at home. The Devils are 8-5-0, third in the Metropolitan, 7-3-0 in their last ten, but only 2-3-0 on the road, and they come in off a loss.

This one should be about shots and special teams. New Jersey shoots as much as anyone, 32.6 shots a game (second in the league), while Vegas is among the league's best at keeping shots away and at limiting dangerous ones. The Devils' power play is fifth in our ratings and Vegas' kill is first, so something has to give when Vegas goes to the box, and the Golden Knights draw more penalties than they take by a wider margin than anyone. Watch the Vegas defensemen: the Golden Knights get more of their shots from the blue line than almost anyone, the Devils far fewer.

Jack Eichel leads Vegas with 20 points, Mark Stone has eight goals, and Mitch Marner has a point in eight straight games. For New Jersey, Jack Hughes has 14 points and Jesper Bratt 11, and Arseny Gritsyuk has five points in his last five games. Brayden McNabb is set to play his 900th NHL game.

The Golden Knights are newly without Carter Hart, so Adin Hill (5-1-0, .895) is expected to start. The Devils are expected to go with Connor Hellebuyck, who is 1-0-0 with a .944 save percentage this season. New Jersey has played better than its record says, and its goaltending has held it back.

At home, Ryan Craig likes to send Tomáš Hertl's line, with Trevor Connelly and Braeden Bowman, out against the other team's fourth line. The Devils can still swing it if they keep the shots coming.
