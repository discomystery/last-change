# The Flames go for a first win over the Kraken

The Kraken have won both meetings this season, each by 6-1, once in Calgary and once in Seattle. Seattle comes in at 8-4-1, third in the Pacific, and has gone 6-3-1 in its last ten. The Flames are 5-7-0, sixth in the Pacific, and have scored only 2.33 goals a game, 30th in the league, while allowing 3.75, 31st.

Expect a fast game. Both teams play at one of the league's quickest paces, Calgary second and Seattle tenth. The Flames like to shoot, but mostly from distance: no team averages a lower chance of scoring per shot. The Kraken give up more shot attempts than any team in the league and 31.1 shots on goal a game, yet the shots they allow are the least dangerous in the league. Seattle's penalty kill is a weak spot, 31st in our ratings at 67.6%, and Calgary's power play has been scoring on 29% of its chances, fifth in the league.

Chandler Stephenson leads Seattle with 11 points and has a point in six straight games against Calgary. Berkly Catton has six points in his last five games, and Brandon Montour is two goals from 100. Joel Farabee leads the Flames with 11 points, seven of them in his last five games, and Matt Coronato and Matvei Gridin have six points apiece over that stretch. The Kraken are newly without Jared McCann. The Flames are newly without Devin Cooley, and have been without Samuel Honzek, Maxim Tsyplakov, Joel Hanley and Connor Zary for several games.

With Cooley out, Dustin Wolf is expected to start for Calgary. He is 1-4-0 with an .839 save percentage this season, and has stopped only .855 of the shots in six starts against Seattle since 2023-24, though a goalie's record against one team says little about the next meeting.

At home, Ryan Huska likes to send his top line of Farabee, Mikael Backlund and Coronato out against the other team's top line, so watch them against Bobby McMann, Matty Beniers and Jordan Eberle. The Flames can tip it if they keep the shots coming.
