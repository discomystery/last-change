# The Canadiens put a four-game run on the line against the Penguins

The Canadiens have won four straight and are 8-3-2, second in the Atlantic. The Penguins are 5-6-2, sixth in the Metropolitan, and only 1-5-1 on the road. They won the first meeting this season, 6-5 in overtime in Pittsburgh on Oct 3.

The Penguins pick their spots: they take more of their shots from in close than any team in the league, and their average shot is the second most dangerous. That is a problem for a Montréal team that ranks 29th at keeping dangerous shots away. The Penguins are also the least physical team in the league, last in hits and in forecheck pressure. Montréal's games get chippy, and the Canadiens take more penalties than they draw by the widest margin in the league, while their own power play has scored on only 10.3% of its chances, 30th.

Montréal will be without Nick Suzuki, Ivan Demidov and Samuel Montembeault, and newly without Mike Matheson, so Cole Caufield (eight goals, 12 points) and Lane Hutson (11 points) carry more of the load. Phillip Danault is set to play his 800th NHL game, and Chris Kreider has six goals in nine games against Pittsburgh since 2023-24, most of them with the Rangers. For the Penguins, Erik Karlsson leads the way with 13 points and Evgeni Malkin has six points in his last five games. Pittsburgh is newly without Bryan Rust and has been without Trevor van Riemsdyk since Oct 9.

Jacob Fowler has been terrific for Montréal, 6-1-0 with a .942 save percentage and a 1.54 goals-against average, and is expected to start again. Artūrs Šilovs (5-3-1, .889) should go for Pittsburgh.

At home, Martin St. Louis likes to send Noah Dobson and Hutson out against the other team's top line, so expect them to see plenty of Sidney Crosby, Tommy Novak and Rickard Rakell. The Canadiens can tip it if they get good looks.
